NearbyNudge: three directions for the redesign

Each mock-up is the review desk, the screen you spend most time on, built with the sample campaign's own data (synthetic businesses). Colors and typefaces are the real ones; the layouts are sketches of the direction, not the finished screens.

Dealt by the roll

Before the web, salespeople found every business on a street in the criss-cross city directory. NearbyNudge prints a fresh one for each campaign: every business in range, in order, with what it says about itself and the letter worth sending.

Palette

Directory white#FFFFFF Ink#000000 Edge red#C8102E Leader grey#A6A6A6 Night red#FF5C61

Type

Archivo only, one family across widths: extra condensed black for running heads, thumb tabs and big numbers; normal width for entries and letters; italic for occupations, like a directory's abbreviations.

Signature

The thumb index: status views die-cut into the list's edge like the tabs on a directory, the open one in red, counts updating as research lands. Every entry is a white-pages line, with dot leaders running to its fit score.

Motion

One move: when you approve, skip or send, the entry folds out of its section and the tab it went to ticks up once. Nothing else animates.

Raised by the challengers it beat

  • From the star atlas: fit is one weight ramp everywhere (Strong heavy, Good medium, Weak light), no badges.
  • From the record sleeve: only the open entry and the open tab are red; nothing is labelled twice.
  • From the developer console: Do not contact stands alone, dashed, away from Approve.
  • From the data field: two inks only, black and red. No grey text: weight, width and italic do the work.

Honest risk

Square corners and ruled columns sit close to the broadsheet look that reads as AI-made; the directory's own devices (leaders, die-cut tabs, condensed heads) have to carry it. Under-40s know it as "the phone book", not by name.

Palette

Midnight enamel#0F1E3D Porcelain#F4F6F5 Scarlet line#EF4B3B Cobalt line#5A88FF Amber line#F5A623 Signal green#2DBE6C

Type

Signika only: a humanist sans drawn for wayfinding signs. Fixed sizes like station labels, mixed case, figures that line up.

Signature

The live line. Each campaign's businesses ride it station by station while research runs. The Campaigns page becomes the network: every campaign is a line through the same stations, with counts at each stop.

Motion

When a business changes status, its dot glides along the line to the next station. With reduced motion it simply appears there.

From the product

Lines take colors in a fixed order (scarlet, cobalt, amber, green) and keep them; a finished campaign goes grey. The route bullets are colored circles on purpose: they're how each line is named.

Honest risk

It carries progress and status brilliantly but adds little to reading drafts and checking facts. A dark-first look needs an equally designed daylight version.

3. Galley proof

My pick

Every draft is a galley proof on the fact-checker's desk. Each claim is marked against the line of the business's own website it came from, and nothing goes out until it's initialed OK.

Palette

Proof white#FFFFFF Graphite#262A30 Blue pencil#1F5FBF Red pencil#CC2E2E Highlighter#FFE45C Margin grey#D5D9DE

Type

Piazzolla for everything you read (the draft, business names, headings), a sharp text serif made for newspapers. Libre Franklin, the newsroom gothic, for controls, labels and the margin.

Signature

The margin. Every highlighted claim has a blue-pencil leader line to the quote it came from, set as a slip beside that sentence. Anything the editor questioned gets a red-pencil underline and its query in the margin, as on this draft.

Motion

Approving writes a red-pencil OK with your initials and the time in the margin, then the next proof slides in. Leader lines draw when you focus a claim.

Why it's my pick

It's the only direction where the look is the product's promise: you can see, line by line, that the email only says what the business says about itself.

Honest risk

The most familiar of the three. Serif-on-white editorial is a common AI look, so it lives or dies on the margin system and on restraint everywhere else.

Also dealt, not shown full size

Vertical video feed (held one test)

One draft fills the screen, actions stacked on the right, the next lead one swipe away. Everyone knows it, but it hides the sources column the product is built on. Worth borrowing for the phone layout only.

Star atlas, record sleeve, developer console, data field (declined)

Each lost on recognition and on clarity. What each did better became a rule in Criss-cross, listed under "Raised by the challengers it beat".
